**Strongest Point FOR the Argument:** [C1] Per-segment ENU frames anchored in float64 ECEF, with grade taken from each sample's own geodetic height, rest on arithmetic that checks out: d^2/2R gives 126 m of drop at 40 km, d/R gives a 0.63 % grade bias at 40 km, and float32 spacing at 512-1024 m is 6.1e-5 m (the "0.06 mm" in OUTPUT-DESIGN section 2). The STATUS.md "own finding" and all three research files reached the same conclusion, and the design keeps the fix cheap: a rigid transform per segment plus a floating origin, which every large-world engine already does. Take seriously because: unlike most of the design, this claim has no unmeasured inputs, and getting it wrong would silently feed a distance-proportional grade error to the trainer.

**Strongest Point AGAINST the Argument:** [C3] The rider path is the RTK antenna track "minus the measured antenna height above the road", and section 4 says it "registers with the splats by construction" because COLMAP is aligned to the same RTK frame. That only holds if (a) the antenna sits vertically above the contact patch, which is false whenever the rig pitches or rolls (the antenna is the highest point on the rig, so it amplifies sway: 5 deg of roll on a 1.5 m mast moves it about 13 cm sideways, which is the same order as the 0.1 m spline tolerance), and (b) the RTK track and the photogrammetric scene agree everywhere, which fails on float stretches (field test 1: 252 of 1415 epochs float, about 18 %, PIPELINE-HANDOFF field test 1) where SfM stays internally consistent but the antenna track can be off by decimetres. Section 4 rejects camera centres because they "carry rig wobble", but the antenna carries the same wobble with a longer lever arm. Problematic because: the visible symptom is the rider floating above, sinking into or drifting across the road exactly where RTK is weakest, and the QA gate in section 7 can detect it but the design has no source of truth to fall back on.

**Further weaknesses:**

[C9, C7, hidden: 80 km capture economics] At the 2 m/s ceiling an 80 km route is about 11 h per pass (section 9), and 2-3 lateral passes make it 22-33 h of capture, plus return legs, all wanted under overcast light. Recommended change: use field test 2 to measure effective capture throughput (km of finished corridor per hour, including passes and turnarounds) and make an explicit go/no-go on the 20-80 km scope; promote cross-session appearance harmonisation (per-session appearance embeddings, joins at natural breaks, a seam QA metric) from "nothing extra in MVP" to a tracked design item, and evaluate a higher frame-rate camera to lift the 2 m/s ceiling.

Recommended change: measure `navigator.storage.estimate()` and `navigator.storage.persist()` on a Quest 3 now (it needs no field data), and add a per-route byte budget per tier to route.json and a QA gate on bytes per km, so the design is constrained by a measured cache rather than an assumed one.

Recommended change: make PPK from `receiver.ubx` plus `rtcm.bin` a standard reprocessing stage once MSM content is verified (PIPELINE-HANDOFF open question 8), bridge float gaps using the SfM road surface constrained by the fixed epochs on either side, and add a QA gate on the longest float gap and the maximum grade step inside it.
