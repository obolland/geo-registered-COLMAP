**IN-2 ANALYSIS:**

**Strongest Point FOR the Argument:** [C1] Per-segment ENU frames anchored in float64 ECEF, with grade taken from each sample's own geodetic height, are correct regardless of anything else in the design. Without them a single origin gives 126 m of curvature drop at 40 km, up to 0.6 % grade bias that the trainer would actually apply, and 4-8 mm float32 resolution at 40-80 km (STATUS.md "Own finding"; OUTPUT-DESIGN.md section 2), and the fix is cheap, standard and renderer-independent. Take seriously because: it is the one decision that is expensive to retrofit once splats are trained and packaged in a frame, and it costs almost nothing at 1 km.

**Strongest Point AGAINST the Argument:** [C9, C7, with C4/C5 corridor data] The uncomfortable truth is that the design is specified for 20-80 km routes with a steerable corridor, and the capture it depends on is not viable at that scale. The handoff puts 1 km at about 1250 triggers, 5000 images and 38 GB (PIPELINE-HANDOFF.md line 222), so one 80 km pass is about 100k triggers per camera, 400k images and about 3 TB, taking about 11 hours at the 2 m/s ceiling (OUTPUT-DESIGN.md section 9). C9 asks for 2-3 same-day overcast passes for a wider corridor, which is 22-33 hours of capture, but C7 only reconstructs passes from one session (or within about an hour) together. By the design's own rules, any real route therefore gets one pass and a corridor of about +-1 m. Problematic because: the riser steering, the corridor channels and the 80 km scaling machinery all assume data the capture process cannot produce. The economics are an unexamined hidden assumption, and they belong in section 9 or section 10 as an open decision, not as a footnote for Olly.

**Further weaknesses (up to 3):**

1. [C8, C5] "Quest 3 baseline" and "ultra-realism is the selling point" pull against each other, and the design leaves that unresolved by marking the numbers **Measure**. The only on-device evidence is about 18 ms per million splats, and a 1M budget runs at 30.7 fps against a 72 Hz target (tiling-lod-and-training.md line 48, citing supersplat-viewer PR #322). That implies roughly 0.3-0.5M splats per frame, and the research itself says the 5k splats/m Quest cap "blurs the road texture near the wheel, which is what the rider looks at" (tiling-lod-and-training.md line 132). Recommended change: make the 1 km MVP an explicit go/no-go for Quest as the baseline, with a perceptual quality bar written down before measuring. Then state in section 10 what happens if Quest fails it, for example PCVR becomes the product tier and Quest becomes a "preview" tier.

2. [C3, C6] "Registers with the splats by construction" is a hope, not a property. Field test 1 had 252 of 1415 PVT epochs in RTK float over just 356 m (PIPELINE-HANDOFF.md line 206). The cameras expose up to about 36 ms apart (handoff line 305). COLMAP fixed pose-prior alignment and scale bugs for multi-camera rigs only in 4.2.0, while 4.1.1 is installed locally (tiling-lod-and-training.md line 101; STATUS.md). The audit also found `alignment_max_error 3` too loose. The section 7 gate names a "path-to-splat check" with no tolerance. Recommended change: set a numeric gate now (for example, road surface under the path within X cm, per-camera prior residuals within Y cm on fixed epochs). Also specify a fallback for when it fails, such as taking `u_road` from the reconstructed road surface instead of antenna height minus a constant.

3. [Hidden assumption: field test 2 validates route-scale decisions] At 1 km, the MVP is a single segment captured at walking pace. It never tests a segment join, a floating-origin re-base, a cross-session seam, LOD popping at 18 m/s on real streaming, or headset storage for a 12-96 GB pre-downloaded route. Browser storage quota on Quest is itself UNVERIFIED (tiling-lod-and-training.md lines 119 and 145; OUTPUT-DESIGN.md section 6 leans on "download before riding"). Recommended change: cut the 1 km MVP into 3-4 segments (about 250-400 m) so joins and re-basing are exercised. Also build a synthetic 80 km package by repeating the MVP tiles, to measure Quest Browser quota, eviction and download time before the package layout in C2 is frozen.

**Overall Assessment:** The design is sound engineering for a 1 km demo and gets the coordinates right, but it quietly encodes an 80 km, steerable, Quest-first product. Its capture cost, corridor width and Quest fidelity are all unproven, and the "Measure" labels postpone exactly the questions that could invalidate the product rather than the pipeline.
