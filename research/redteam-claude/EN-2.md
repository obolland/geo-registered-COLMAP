**EN-2 ANALYSIS:**

**Strongest Point FOR the Argument:** [C1] The coordinate design is one of the few parts that already survives its own worst case: at 80 km a single ENU frame is 126 m below the ground at 40 km, biases grade by about 0.6 % and loses float32 resolution to about 8 mm (STATUS.md "Own finding"), and per-segment frames with float64 ECEF anchors plus grade from each sample's own geodetic height remove all three failure modes at once rather than shrinking them (OUTPUT-DESIGN.md section 2). It also records the input datum explicitly instead of assuming WGS84, which closes the POC's silent-datum bug (STATUS.md audit, `enu_origin.json`). Take seriously because: when I asked "what if the route is 80 km, or the corrections are not WGS84?", the design already has a named answer for each, which is not true of any other claim.

**Strongest Point AGAINST the Argument:** [C5, also C1/C2] Every partitioning rule in the design (segments, training blocks, ownership trim, LOD chunks, prefetch) is indexed by route arclength, which silently assumes that arclength distance and 3D distance agree, i.e. that the route never comes back near itself. That is false on exactly the terrain the product sells: switchback descents (the 40 mph case), out-and-back legs, figure-of-eight and loop routes (start meets end), and the 2-3 lateral passes C9 asks for; there, a splat on the lower leg of a hairpin is 20-30 m in 3D from block A but 300 m in arclength from it, so the "nearest chunk" trim (tiling-lod-and-training.md section 4, H3DGS rule) cuts through the middle of separately trained, differently lit geometry with no training overlap, cameras "chosen by what they see" put the same patch in two blocks, two segments' `bounds_local` overlap so both get loaded and the same surface is drawn twice (the duplication runtime-rendering-and-headsets.md item 2 says must not happen, on a device where overdraw is already the dominant cost, ibid. item 3), and on an out-and-back the facing leg belongs to a segment 10 km away that prefetch has not loaded. Problematic because: the failure is invisible on field test 2's straight 1 km walk, so the design would be "validated" and then break on the first real alpine or Devon-lane descent.

**Further weaknesses (up to 3):**

1. [C3] "Registers with the splats by construction" is only true when the RTK track is the truth and the antenna is vertically above the road contact, and neither holds in common cases. The antenna is on a handlebar bar of a heavy e-bike "ridden or walked" (PIPELINE-HANDOFF.md, Mount), so it leans with the bike and swings with steering: at roughly 1.2 m antenna height, a 5 degree lean moves it about 0.10 m sideways and 10 degrees about 0.21 m, as a one-sided bias if the bike is always walked from the same side, which already exceeds the 0.1 m path tolerance and is not caught by the vertical "road surface under the path" QA check. Separately, field test 1 on an open 356 m walk was RTK float for 252 of 1415 epochs (18 %, PIPELINE-HANDOFF.md, Field test 1); under tree canopy, in cuttings, under bridges or in tunnels the track goes float or none while COLMAP, with down-weighted priors, follows the images, so the path and the splats disagree by decimetres exactly where it matters. Recommended change: derive the road trace from the reconstructed rig pose (aligned rotation plus the lever arm, the same machinery as handoff ingest step 6) projected along true gravity, use raw RTK only as the prior and the drift check, and add a QA gate on lateral path offset against the splat-derived road surface and lane edges, not just vertical.

2. [C7, C9] The capture arithmetic makes multi-session the normal case for any real route, not an edge to defer. 80 km at the 2 m/s ceiling is 11 hours per pass (OUTPUT-DESIGN.md section 9), longer than a UK winter day, and 2-3 same-day lateral passes is 22-33 hours, while section 8 only merges passes "within about an hour" (about 7 km of riding). So a real route is necessarily many sessions, the lateral passes have to be shuttles of about 1 km (out, turn, back on the other side of the road with cameras facing the other way), and appearance seams occur every few kilometres; "nothing extra is needed beyond the provenance field" leaves the dominant visual risk undesigned. Recommended change: capture field test 2 deliberately as two sessions over the same 1 km (different times, one shuttle lateral pass) and make the cross-session seam (shared appearance across an overlap block, fixed reference exposure, seam placement rule) a measured MVP gate rather than an open risk.

3. [C6, C2] The QA gates and manifest have no representation for the topology edge cases above, so they cannot fail loudly on them. Recommended change: beyond the existing `loop` flag, add self-proximity metadata (pairs of segments whose bounds come within a set distance in 3D), assign splat ownership by 3D proximity to the capturing cameras rather than arclength alone, make prefetch and the splat budget work over 3D neighbourhoods instead of the next N segments, and add a QA gate for duplicated splats across segment bounds, plus a synthetic hairpin and out-and-back fixture to the test plan.

**Overall Assessment:** The coordinate and data-contract choices are sound for the long-route edge case they were designed for, but the design is quietly one-dimensional (arclength, single pass, RTK as truth, upright antenna), and the routes the product exists to sell (descents, loops, wooded lanes, multi-day captures) are where each of those assumptions breaks, none of which field test 2 as planned will reveal.
