**AR-1 ANALYSIS:**

**Strongest Point FOR the Argument:** [C1] Per-segment ENU frames on a float64 ECEF anchor, with grade taken from each sample's own geodetic height, buy correctness for almost nothing: the only cost is a rigid re-base at each 1 km boundary, which the app needs anyway for its floating origin. Every runtime format the design might ship stores positions in a bounded local range (SOG 16 bits per axis over a per-file range, Spark float16 centres at 0.1 % relative precision, SPZ 24-bit fixed point; runtime-rendering-and-headsets.md finding 6 and section 3 table), so file-local origins are forced by the formats, and a single "up" axis would put a 0.6 % grade bias into the trainer at 40 km (STATUS.md "Own finding"). Take seriously because: three research topics reached it independently from verified encoding specs, and it is one of the few decisions here whose cost is genuinely negligible.

**Strongest Point AGAINST the Argument:** [C5, with C8] The Quest tier cap of "near 5k splats per metre" trades the selling point (ultra-realism on the baseline headset most riders will own) for a storage and bandwidth constraint that nobody has measured, and it expresses the cap in the wrong unit. The 5k figure comes from bytes, not frame time (tiling-lod-and-training.md finding 10: 5k/15k/40k splats/m gives 12/36/96 GB per 80 km), while the frame budget (0.3-0.8M rendered splats at 72 Hz, runtime finding 2 and tiling section 5) would allow a denser finest level within the ~30 m that is actually rendered at LOD0; the binding constraint, Quest Browser storage quota and eviction for 10-40 GB, is explicitly UNVERIFIED (tiling line 119 and risk table "Browser storage for pre-caching"). A per-route-metre cap also spreads density evenly across a 60-100 m corridor, whereas the research itself notes that a lower Quest density "blurs the road texture near the wheel, which is what the rider looks at" (tiling section 6 trade-offs). Problematic because: you gain a predictable 12 GB download but lose near-field fidelity on the baseline device, and near-field fidelity is both the product's differentiator and, through vection and floaters, part of its comfort story.

**Further weaknesses (up to 3):**

1. [C4] `grade_ctrl` and `comfort_speed_mps` bake tunable policy into the published data while the design claims device and user policy belong in the app. The trainer research says the control window should be "tuned by speed" and rate-limited in %/s (trainer-and-sensors.md line 171), both time-domain quantities a distance-indexed curve cannot carry, trainer response times are unknown for every brand including the dev unit (trainer line 76, test 5), and the comfort budget is a per-rider sickness tolerance (comfort-and-sickness.md lines 85-86, 129). You gain one authoritative curve; you lose the ability to retune without republishing every route. Change: publish the physical primitives (h_ortho, sigma_h, curvature) as the contract, ship grade_ctrl and comfort_speed only as defaults with their parameters recorded in route.json, and put the reference smoothing code in a shared library the app can rerun per device, speed and rider.

2. [C7, with C9] The multi-session rule gains independent, re-capturable segments but makes visible appearance seams structural rather than exceptional. At the ~2 m/s ceiling an 80 km pass takes about 11 hours (section 9), and 2-3 lateral passes multiply that, so a real route spans several days and many hours of changing light; C7 merges only "within about an hour", so adjacent segments captured hours or days apart meet at a hard trimmed cut with no shared training overlap, and the 1 km single-session MVP cannot observe this at all. Change: make the cross-time join a first-class QA gate now (colour and geometry continuity across segment boundaries, including road-surface height offset), allow a cross-session overlap block trained with per-session appearance embeddings at joins, and add capture guidance to end each session past a planned join point so the next session re-covers 50-100 m.

3. [C3] "Registers with the splats by construction" trades a clean, comfortable rider path for dependence on an alignment accuracy that has not been demonstrated. The path is the RTK antenna track minus a measured antenna height, smoothed within 0.1 m, while the splats sit wherever COLMAP's aligned reconstruction puts the road; the POC used `alignment_max_error 3` (metres), flagged as "too loose for RTK" (STATUS.md audit), and the lever-arm re-alignment loop in PIPELINE-HANDOFF.md (rig config slice, lines 295-297) is still unbuilt. A few centimetres of vertical disagreement puts the rider visibly into or above the road, which is the most-looked-at surface. Change: treat the path-to-road check in section 7 as the primary registration metric with a stated tolerance, and give the path a per-segment vertical correction fitted to the reconstructed road surface (bounded, logged in QA) rather than assuming the two agree.

**Overall Assessment:** The coordinate and packaging skeleton is sound and cheap, but the design repeatedly locks in trade-offs (Quest density by route metre, comfort and grade policy in the data, segment independence over continuity) on the basis of constraints the 1 km walking-speed MVP will not measure, and the costs land on realism and comfort, the two things the product depends on.
