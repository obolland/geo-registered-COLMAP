**EN-1 ANALYSIS:**

**Strongest Point FOR the Argument:** [C1] The coordinate case is the one part of the design whose numbers I could check independently, and they all hold: tangent-plane drop d^2/2R = 125.6 m at 40 km, float32 ulp at 40 km = 2^-23 x 32768 = 3.9 mm and at 600 m about 0.06 mm, inter-segment rotation 1/6371 rad = 0.009 deg per km (OUTPUT-DESIGN section 2, STATUS "Own finding"). The grade claim behind it is also supported by real data: in field test 1 (`gps-camera-trigger/samples/20261108T153731Z/pvt.csv`, 1415 epochs at 5 Hz) RTK-fixed epochs have vAcc median 13 mm, p95 36 mm, and fixed-only height residual against a 5-epoch moving average has sd 20 mm, consistent with the "3 cm vertical noise" assumption in section 5. Take seriously because: it is arithmetic plus measured receiver output, not inference, and it rules out the single-origin alternative by a margin of orders of magnitude.

**Strongest Point AGAINST the Argument:** [C8, C5] The Quest tier, the 5k splats/m cap and the PlayCanvas choice rest on no road-scene headset measurement; the research itself says "Published Quest 3 measurements with methodology: none found" (runtime-rendering section 3), and the 5k/m cap is derived from bandwidth (tiling section 7 table), not from frame time or visual quality. The one real number, supersplat-viewer PR #322, implies a large fixed cost: 30.7 fps at 1M (32.6 ms) minus 18 ms/M leaves about 14.6 ms of non-splat cost in quality mode, already over the 13.9 ms 72 Hz budget; performance mode (40.3 fps, 24.8 ms) leaves about 7 ms, i.e. roughly 0.39M splats at 72 Hz and 0.24M at 90 Hz if the slope is linear (tiling section 3; my arithmetic). Problematic because: "frame rate as high as possible" and "ultra-realism" are the product's two headline requirements, and the evidence on file suggests 72 Hz is marginal and 90-120 Hz may be out of reach on the WebGL2 path, which would change the tier design, not just its numbers.

**Further weaknesses (up to 3):**

1. [C9, C7, hidden assumption on capture economics] The design's own numbers make its multi-session rule unworkable at product scale. An 80 km pass is 11.1 h at 2 m/s (section 9); 2-3 lateral passes is 22-33 h; field test 2 is "about 5000 images and about 38 GB" per km (PIPELINE-HANDOFF section 4), so about 3 TB per 80 km pass and 6-9 TB for a corridor-width capture. Yet section 8 merges only passes "within about an hour", so almost every real route is multi-session and a corridor wider than about +-1 m is effectively unavailable on long routes. Recommended change: treat cross-session joins and per-segment pass count as first-class from v1 (a `passes` and `corridor_source` field per segment, and a QA metric for appearance discontinuity at joins), and add an explicit capture-cost model (hours, TB, GPU-hours per km) to be filled in from field test 2 before committing to 20-80 km routes.

2. [C3, hidden assumption on registration] "The path and the splats register by construction" (section 4) is asserted, not measured: field test 1 has no images (handoff section 4), the POC aligned with `alignment_max_error 3` (STATUS audit), and "residuals at cm level on fixed epochs" is slice 6's acceptance target, not a result (handoff slice table). The lever-arm chain also carries known error terms: per-camera exposure skew up to about 36 ms (about 29 cm at 8 m/s) and an antenna position not yet decided (handoff sections 2, 6). Recommended change: give the section 7 registration gate a numeric threshold (for example per-camera prior-vs-reconstructed p95 and path-to-road-surface p95 in cm) and make C3 conditional on field test 2 meeting it, with a fallback (path fitted to the reconstructed road surface) if it does not.

3. [C4, C6] The grade design quantifies only the fixed-RTK case; the measured data says outages are common and long. Field test 1 was 18 % non-fixed (252 float, 3 none of 1415 epochs) in 6 runs, the longest 103 epochs (about 21 s, about 41 m at the 2 m/s capture ceiling), with float vAcc median 38 mm, p95 124 mm (my analysis of `pvt.csv`). A 41 m gap is longer than the whole 10-25 m `grade_ctrl` window, so "float sections get flagged and smoothed harder" (section 5) has no stated error bound for what the rider will feel. Recommended change: add a QA gate on maximum non-fixed run length in metres and on worst-case `grade_ctrl` error across it, and test PPK from the logged `rtcm.bin` (MSM availability is unverified per handoff section 4) on field test 1 before field test 2, since it is the cheapest way to shrink these gaps.

**Overall Assessment:** The geometric core (C1, and C4 on fixed RTK) is backed by checkable numbers, but the decisions that determine whether the product feels real and comfortable on a Quest (tier budgets, renderer, registration, capture scale) are inferences with no measurement on file, and the only real dataset already shows that long float gaps and multi-session routes are the normal case rather than the exception.
