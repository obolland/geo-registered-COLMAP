**IN-1 ANALYSIS:**

**Strongest Point FOR the Argument:** [C1] Ask "why not one ENU origin?" and you get a clear answer with numbers that hold up: curvature drop d^2/2R is about 126 m at 40 km, a single "up" axis biases grade by about d/R (0.6 % at 40 km), and the trainer would apply that bias directly (STATUS.md "Own finding"; OUTPUT-DESIGN section 2). Every splat container stores positions with limited precision relative to a local range: SOG uses 16-bit per axis over a per-file range, and Spark uses float16 centres (runtime-rendering research item 6). So local frames with a float64 anchor and a floating origin are needed whatever renderer is chosen. Computing grade from each sample's own geodetic height also removes the frame from the physics completely. Take seriously because: three research threads reached it independently, and it is the one decision that would be expensive to change after routes are published.

**Strongest Point AGAINST the Argument:** [C4] Why does the pipeline publish `grade_ctrl` (a 10-25 m zero-phase window "Measure on the KICKR CORE 2") and `comfort_speed_mps` (against a "Measure" budget) when every input that sets them is only known at runtime? Those inputs are the rider's speed, the specific trainer's response time and the user's comfort tolerance. The design's own evidence gives the control filter in speed and time terms: "tune by speed" and "rate-limited to a few %/s" (trainer-and-sensors.md section 9 item 3). A fixed 25 m distance window lasts 5.5 s at 10 mph but 1.4 s at 40 mph, and the time-based rate limit was dropped from OUTPUT-DESIGN section 5 because a distance-indexed file can't express it. The design also promises "wide trainer support" while tuning on one unit, even though response behaviour has "no first-party figure" for any brand (trainer research, response-time row). Problematic because: these are tuning parameters, and the design publishes them as versioned route data, so every retune for a new trainer or comfort budget means rebuilding and republishing every route. Recommended change: publish the raw ingredients (`h_ortho`, `sigma_h`, a lightly smoothed canonical `grade_geom`, and `curvature`). Keep one canonical pipeline grade only if fairness between riders or on leaderboards needs an identical grade. Move the control filter, rate limit and comfort speed into an app module parameterised by device and user. Also drop `yaw_rate_per_mps`: yaw rate = v x curvature, so this channel is just `curvature` again under a second name.

**Further weaknesses (up to 3):**

1. [C3] Why does the antenna track "register by construction" with the splats? Step 1 of section 4 takes the antenna position "minus the measured antenna height above the road". That is a scalar subtraction that ignores the rig's attitude. The handoff says the rig is a bar on the handlebar of a heavy e-bike (PIPELINE-HANDOFF.md "Mount"), so it yaws with every steering input and leans with the bike. With the antenna about 1.2 m up, a 3 degree lean moves the contact point about 6 cm sideways, which uses up most of the 0.1 m path budget before any smoothing. Registration itself also depends on things not yet verified: COLMAP 4.1.1 has the pose-prior rig bugs fixed in 4.2.0 (tiling research item 9), and the POC used `alignment_max_error 3` (STATUS.md audit). Change: compute the road contact point with the aligned `R_world_from_rig` and the full lever arm, as handoff step 6 already does for the cameras. Then make the section 7 "path-to-splat" check a numeric gate (for example, road surface under the path within 5 cm) and stop assuming the path and splats register.

2. [C1, C2, C5] Why should field test 2 settle segment length (Open decision 2) when the 1 km MVP is a single ~1 km segment? With one segment, nothing in the test exercises re-basing between segments, the transform at a join, LOD chunks or training blocks that cross a segment edge, or how 100 m blocks, 25-50 m overlaps and 16 m chunks nest inside a segment boundary. Those are exactly the mechanisms that make 80 km work, and the MVP would leave all of them untested. Change: cut the MVP deliberately into 3-4 segments of about 250-300 m. Ride a fly-through across the joins with the floating origin re-basing, so the multi-segment code paths and seam QA get exercised on day one.

3. [C2, C5, hidden assumption] Why assume a route can be downloaded before riding? The design's own source puts the Quest tier at 12 GB for 80 km even at the 5k splats/m cap, and 36 GB at 15k (tiling-lod-and-training.md section 7 table). The Quest Browser storage quota and eviction policy are UNVERIFIED (same file, line 119 and the open-questions table). If the quota is a few GB, the density cap, the tier split and the package granularity all change, so this decides the shape of the package rather than being a later app detail. Change: run the Quest Browser quota and eviction test (Cache API, OPFS) before fixing the splats/m cap. Add `bytes` per segment and tier to `route.json` so the app can plan pre-caching and show download size.

**Overall Assessment:** The coordinate frame and package skeleton survive repeated "why" questions. The design weakens where it bakes runtime tuning (control grade, comfort speed) into published data, assumes the path registers with the splats instead of measuring it, and plans to validate an 80 km segmented design on a single-segment MVP.
