**PT-2 ANALYSIS:**

**Strongest Point FOR the Argument:** [C2, with C1] The package treats every published byte as a derived product of unpublished lossless masters (per-block float32 PLY, the COLMAP model, the run log) and stores geometry in a renderer-neutral ENU-plus-float64-ECEF frame, converting axes only at runtime (OUTPUT-DESIGN 2, 3). Against the most predictable adversary here, platform vendors, that is the right hedge: when PlayCanvas churns its LOD API, SOG is revised, Meta ships WebGPU XR on Quest, or the Spark bake-off wins, the response is to rebuild packages from the masters, not to recapture roads. Take seriously because: the churn is already on record: PlayCanvas added error-based LOD in 2.22 and removed it in 2.23, Spark fixed XR splats rendered at half width only in 2.4.0 (Oct 2026), and WebGPU-in-WebXR is still experimental on Quest Browser 152.1 (runtime-rendering 1, 3).

**Strongest Point AGAINST the Argument:** [C3] "The path and the splats register by construction" (OUTPUT-DESIGN 4) holds only if the RTK trace and the reconstructed scene agree locally. In fact the path comes straight from the antenna track, while the splats sit in a COLMAP model fitted to that track by an alignment with residuals. The physical world breaks that agreement exactly where it matters. Field test 1 was in RTK float for 252 of 1415 epochs (18%) while walking (PIPELINE-HANDOFF, field test 1), and a 51.16 N, -4.12 E route (North Devon, the route.json example) means hedged, tree-lined lanes where multipath and canopy cause float or no fix. In those stretches the bundle-adjusted visual poses are more accurate than the RTK trace, so a path built from RTK will float above, sink into or slide across the reconstructed road by decimetres. Problematic because: a path that disagrees with the visible road surface is a comfort defect, not a QA footnote, and section 7 can detect it but the design gives no correct source to fall back to.

**Further weaknesses:**

1. [C3] Subtracting a fixed "antenna height above the road" (OUTPUT-DESIGN 4 step 1, 9) assumes the antenna stays vertically above the contact patch. The rig is bolted to the handlebar of a heavy e-bike that is ridden or walked (PIPELINE-HANDOFF 3), so the antenna yaws with steering and leans with the bike: at about 1 m height, 5 degrees of lean moves the surface point about 9 cm sideways, which is the same size as the whole 0.1 m smoothing tolerance. Recommended change: build the road trace from the bundle-adjusted rig-frame poses (COLMAP 4.2 rigs give one pose per frame, not four interleaved centres, which removes the stated reason for avoiding them), drop each pose to the road using its full rotation and the rig config lever arm, and take u_road from the reconstructed road surface. RTK then becomes the prior and the QA reference, not the path source.

2. [C9, C7, hidden economics assumption] Weather and daylight make the capture economics adversarial, and the design's own rules interact badly. An 80 km route is about 11 h per pass at the 2 m/s ceiling. With the 2-3 lateral passes needed for steering (C9) that is 22-33 h, but only under overcast skies and with passes merged within about an hour (OUTPUT-DESIGN 8). So one route takes several separate overcast days, with an appearance seam at every day boundary, and probably foliage or road-surface change between them (tiling 4.2, Block-NeRF on seasonal vegetation). Section 8's "nothing extra is needed in the MVP" holds only for the 1 km MVP. A competitor using video on a car (Rouvy and FulGaz style routes, trainer 5) films 80 km in a morning, so the realism advantage is paid for with roughly 10-30x more capture time. Recommended change: make capture days a first-class planning unit now. Add a segment `session_join` flag and an appearance-transition QA metric at each multi-session boundary. Have the capture plan state the expected number of days and seams per route. Treat a steering corridor wider than about +-1 m as a premium option on selected segments rather than the default (C9), so a typical route needs one pass.

3. [C2, C5, hidden assumption that a route is downloaded before riding] The design pushes Quest playback to pre-caching (12-96 GB for 80 km, tiling 7), but the storage is controlled by an adversary the pipeline can't negotiate with. Quest Browser storage quota and eviction are UNVERIFIED (tiling 7, runtime-rendering 2). Chromium treats origin storage as best-effort and evictable under pressure unless `navigator.storage.persist()` is granted (web.dev, "Storage for the web"), and a 128 GB Quest 3 or 3S holding other apps can't hold even the 5k splats/m tier for several long routes. The 5k splats/m Quest cap is therefore likely set by storage and eviction rather than GPU frame time, yet route.json carries no byte sizes the app could plan around. Recommended change: add `bytes` per tier per segment and per LOD level to route.json, and make the segment the unit of caching. Specify a rolling cache (current segment plus N ahead, downloaded during slow sections) as the baseline, with whole-route pre-download as an optimisation. Add one bake-off measurement to section 6: on a real Quest 3, record `navigator.storage.estimate()`, whether `persist()` is granted, and eviction behaviour at 5, 15 and 40 GB.

**Overall Assessment:** The coordinate and packaging design is a sound hedge against platform churn. But the argument underrates the physical world as an adversary: GNSS under canopy, a leaning handlebar antenna, weather-bound multi-day capture and vendor-controlled headset storage each undercut a claim (registration by construction, seamless multi-session joins, download-before-ride) that the 1 km walking-speed MVP is poorly placed to test.
