**AR-2 ANALYSIS:**

**Strongest Point FOR the Argument:** [C1] Per-segment ENU frames on float64 ECEF anchors, with grade taken from each sample's own geodetic height, cut off a whole family of downstream failures before they start. Because grade does not depend on any frame's U axis, segment length, re-segmentation, multi-session joins and a later renderer switch can all change without changing what the rider feels on the trainer. The ECEF anchor is also what makes C7's "join by shared earth anchor" and C2's "regenerate a Spark package from masters" cheap rather than a migration. The numbers check out: a 40 km chord drops d^2/2R = 1.6e9/1.27e7 = about 126 m, and the float32 ulp at 40 km is 2^-8 m = about 3.9 mm, matching OUTPUT-DESIGN section 2. Take seriously because: it is the one decision that is expensive to reverse once routes are published, and it is the right one.

**Strongest Point AGAINST the Argument:** [C9 with C7 and C4 corridor] The capture rules cannot all hold on a real route, and when they break the steering corridor goes with them. At the 2 m/s ceiling an 80 km pass takes 11.1 h (section 9). C9 asks for 2-3 *same-day* lateral passes in overcast light, but C7 and tiling research section 4 ("merge lateral passes captured within about an hour") forbid merging passes from different days. Even with 8 h of usable daylight, three passes fit only about 19 km of route per day, so an 80 km route needs four or more capture days. So: (A) the lateral passes for most of the route land on different days, so (B) C7 forbids merging them, so (C) `corridor_*_m` collapses to about 1 m nearly everywhere, which (D) makes the steering riser in section 1 cosmetic and turns the day boundaries into appearance joins every ~20 km. The design calls those joins "the open risk" and handles them only with a provenance field. On top of that, the PIPELINE-HANDOFF section 3 figure of about 38 GB per km per pass means about 3 TB per pass for 80 km, or 9 TB for three passes, to upload to AWS. Problematic because: field test 2 (1 km, one session, walking pace) is structurally unable to expose this, so the package format will be fixed before the constraint that breaks it is ever hit.

**Further weaknesses (up to 3):**

1. [C5] Seams that are geometrically clean can still show a visible appearance step. Each ~100 m block is trained on its own with per-image `app_opt`/PPISP (tiling research, key finding 7), so each block settles on its own exposure and colour reference. Trimming to ownership intervals handles duplicate splats but does nothing about colour. The result could be a brightness or white-balance step every 100 m, which is every 5.5 s at 18 m/s and spans the whole field of view, the kind of temporal flicker the comfort research ties to sickness. Block-NeRF, which the design cites, explicitly matches neighbouring appearance codes over the overlap (tiling research section 3). Change: fix one appearance reference per session, constrain or match each block's appearance over its overlap, and add a seam colour-delta metric (rendered either side of each cut) as a hard QA gate in C6.

2. [C3, feeds C5 floaters] "Registers by construction" assumes the COLMAP-to-RTK alignment is uniform to cm level along the segment. In fact it is a similarity fit with residuals, plus a lever-arm iteration that is still to be measured (handoff section 6), and the rig skew is up to about 36 ms. Any residual sends errors two ways. The rider's eye floats or sinks against the rendered road, and the floater tube, which is centred on the RTK-derived eye trajectory with a 1.5-2 m radius (section 6), reaches down to the road surface when the seated eye height is about 1.2-1.5 m. If that radius is isotropic it deletes road and kerb splats right under and beside the rider, the most-viewed pixels in the product. Change: define the floater region as clearance above the local splat road surface (an asymmetric volume, not a radius about the eye), and make the section 7 path-to-splat check measure and correct a per-segment vertical and lateral offset before the path and the floater cull are computed.

3. [C4 and C6] Publishing derived policy plus a route-wide hard gate makes every tuning change force a rebuild. `comfort_speed_mps` is a pure function of `curvature` and a comfort budget that is still marked **Measure** and will differ between riders, so every budget retune means republishing every route, or the app ignores the field and the two drift apart. Likewise, one failing segment out of 80 blocks the whole route (section 7, "per route"), and the gate thresholds are themselves unmeasured. The likely chain is that gates are loosened until routes ship, and then the gate is ceremony. Change: publish only curvature and yaw-rate-per-m/s and let the app compute comfort speed (consistent with the design's own "device clamps and difficulty stay in the app"); gate per segment with severity levels, record the threshold set's version in `qa/report.json`, and allow a partial publish with a marked stretch or an explicit recapture list.

**Overall Assessment:** The frame, package and trainer-channel design is sound and reversible where it matters most. The capture constraints, per-block appearance and derived comfort policy each have second-order effects that only show up beyond the 1 km single-session MVP, so those should be designed for now and not left to field test 2 to reveal.
