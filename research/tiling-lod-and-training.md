# Tiling, LOD and training for long-route Gaussian splat streaming

Date of research: 2026-11-12. Labels: VERIFIED = read in the cited primary source on 2026-11-12; INFERRED = my reasoning; UNVERIFIED = not confirmed in a primary source.

## 1 Summary (top 10 findings)

1. Licences decide the stack. Every large-scene 3DGS method with public code (Hierarchical 3DGS, Octree-GS, Momentum-GS, CityGaussian, Mip-Splatting, the 3DGS core of DOGS) is non-commercial; Difix3D+ is NVIDIA research-only; Street Gaussians now needs registration for any organisational use. gsplat, Nerfstudio, Brush, NVIDIA 3DGRUT (Apache 2.0) and Spark, splat-transform (MIT) are commercially usable. Reimplement the ideas (chunking, consolidation, LOD merge) on gsplat. (VERIFIED licences, section 2.)
2. The Quest 3 baseline is splat-bound, not bandwidth-bound: PlayCanvas measured about 18 ms per million splats on Quest 3, and 30.7 fps with a 1M budget in WebXR (PR merged 2026-10-01). At 72 Hz (13.9 ms) that implies roughly 0.3-0.5M rendered splats per frame. (VERIFIED measurement; budget INFERRED.)
3. Hierarchical 3DGS is the reference design for a captured route: 50 m chunks (walking) to 100 m chunks (vehicle), trimmed so each Gaussian belongs to its nearest chunk, a BVH LOD tree with KL-style merges, a pixel-size cut tau, and parent/child interpolation against popping. It was built for a helmet rig of 5-6 GoPros at 6-7 km/h, close to this capture. (VERIFIED.)
4. Web runtimes take ready-made LOD as input: PlayCanvas Streamed SOG (`lod-meta.json`, binary tree, 16 m / 512K-splat chunks by default, built by splat-transform 3.11.2 from per-level PLYs or by decimation), Spark 2.x `.RAD` (LoD tree, HTTP Range streaming, `SparkXr` for WebXR), and Cesium 3D Tiles with glTF `KHR_gaussian_splatting` (ratified) plus SPZ compression. The cheapest path is to train full-detail tiles on gsplat and let splat-transform or Spark `build-lod` make the LOD. (VERIFIED.)
5. Popping control differs: H3DGS interpolates parent and child, Spark uses a 1.5 LoD base for smoother steps, LODGE blends opacity across chunk boundaries; the PlayCanvas docs say nothing about transition blending. That needs a visual test at 18 m/s in a headset. (VERIFIED for each doc; risk INFERRED.)
6. Moving sideways off the captured line is the main quality risk. Driving benchmarks score a 3 m lane shift and plain reconstructions do badly there (Street Gaussians FID 95.3 at 3 m on nuScenes). Diffusion and geometry priors help (ExtraGS 77.2, StreetCrafter 71.4 on Waymo) but do not close the gap. Plan for at least two lateral passes, or limit steering to about +-1 m. (VERIFIED numbers; capture advice INFERRED.)
7. Appearance: gsplat has per-image appearance embeddings (`app_opt`), a bilateral grid, and PPISP (CVPR 2026: exposure, vignetting, colour, CRF, plus a controller that predicts novel-view exposure), and it reads EXIF exposure. Splatfacto (last commit 2025-07-29) has a bilateral grid, MCMC and antialiased mode, but no PPISP, no 3DGUT and no per-image embedding. Train in raw gsplat. (VERIFIED.)
8. Distortion and the focal-plane shutter: 3DGUT in gsplat trains directly on distorted images and models rolling shutter, but only with the MCMC strategy. A DSLR curtain moving during a 1-2 m/s capture is a small rolling shutter. MCMC also gives a hard `cap_max` on splat count, which a streaming budget needs. (VERIFIED for gsplat; shutter effect INFERRED.)
9. Upgrade COLMAP to 4.2.x before relying on rig plus pose priors: 4.2.0 (2026-08-31) fixed "pose-prior alignment and scale preservation for multi-camera rigs", and 4.2.0/4.2.1 fixed rig paths in the global mapper. 4.1.1 has these bugs. (VERIFIED changelog.)
10. Bandwidth (section 7): at an assumed 15 bytes per splat (SOG) with 2x LOD overhead, 5k / 15k / 40k splats per metre of route need 22 / 65 / 173 Mbit/s at 18 m/s, and 12 / 36 / 96 GB for 80 km. So: cap the Quest tier near 5k splats/m, buffer at least 10 s (180 m) ahead, and pre-cache the route before the ride rather than rely on live streaming at downhill speed. (INFERRED; density must be measured on the 1 km MVP.)

## 3 LOD

### 3.1 How LOD levels are built

| Approach | Merge / level construction | Storage | Anti-popping | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H3DGS hierarchy | Binary BVH (median split on the longest axis). Hierarchy is then optimised. Storage about 68% larger than flat 3DGS. | Per-chunk hierarchy files merged by `GaussianHierarchyMerger` | Cut where a node's projected size is below tau (for example 1-6 px) and its parent's is above; interpolate attributes with t = (tau - eps(n)) / (eps(p) - eps(n)) | VERIFIED [arXiv 2406.12080](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12080), [repo](https://github.com/graphdeco-inria/hierarchical-3d-gaussians) |
| LODGE | Per level: depth-aware 3D smoothing (minimum size by Nyquist), importance pruning, then fine-tuning; distance bands d_l picked greedily | Precomputed active set per camera-cluster chunk (about 5 m) | Opacity ramp across chunk boundaries | VERIFIED [arXiv 2505.23158v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23158v2) |

| PlayCanvas Streamed SOG | Either supply per-level splat files (LOD 0 finest) or let splat-transform decimate one PLY (`--decimate`, `--decimate-adaptive`; merged opacity may exceed 1, stored as `opacity_linear` or in SOG v3) | `lod-meta.json` (binary spatial tree; leaves point at file, offset, count; one LOD level per chunk; Morton order) plus SOG chunks (WebP textures). ### 3.2 Runtime notes for VR

- Quest 3 (PlayCanvas supersplat-viewer PR #322, merged 2026-10-01): standalone XR previously got the 4M desktop budget and ran at 9.7 fps; with a 1M budget, a 72 Hz target and fixed foveation (15-20% gain) it ran at 30.7 fps (quality) and 40.3 fps (performance); "each million splats cost about 18 ms per frame" (VERIFIED, [PR #322](https://github.com/playcanvas/supersplat-viewer/pull/322)). Consequence: a 72 Hz Quest 3 frame fits roughly 0.3-0.5M splats (INFERRED).
- Spark defaults to 1.5M splats on desktop and 500K on mobile, with `outsideFoveate` and `behindFoveate` to cut detail outside the frustum and behind the viewer (VERIFIED, [Spark LoD docs](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/new-spark-renderer/)). In VR, behind-viewer splats can be cut hard because the rider looks forward, but head turns must not show holes (INFERRED).
- Positional precision: float32 resolution at 40 km from origin is about 4 mm, so a route-global ENU frame causes jitter in a renderer that keeps world-space float32 centres. Use per-segment local origins (INFERRED). Spark 2 stores float32 centres (VERIFIED, Spark LoD docs); SOG quantises means to 16 bits per axis within each file's bounds (VERIFIED, [SOG spec](https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/gaussian-splatting/formats/sog/)).
- Use antialiased (Mip-style) training if the renderer supports it: splat-transform carries an `antialiased` model tag through SOG/SPZ (VERIFIED, README), and a renderer that ignores it shows the scene slightly wrong (INFERRED).

### 4.2 Capture implications for the rig not yet built (all INFERRED unless marked)

- Capture rate caps speed: one photo every 0.8 m at no more than one every 0.4 s means at most 2 m/s (7.2 km/h) while capturing. That matches H3DGS's 6-7 km/h (VERIFIED for H3DGS). Capture speed and ride speed are unrelated.
- Field of view: an 18 mm lens on the 100D's APS-C sensor (22.3 x 14.9 mm, sensor size UNVERIFIED) gives about 64 x 45 degrees. Four cameras need overlap for COLMAP rig calibration, so they cannot cover 360 degrees. Point them forward-left, forward-right, left and right (or similar), and give them enough upward tilt to see roofs and trees, or the top of the frame will be empty at close range.
- Lateral parallax: a handlebar rig spans about 0.5 m, so every view lies on a line about 0.5 m wide. For a road-width corridor (+-2-3 m), capture 2-3 passes offset across the lane (kerb side, centre, crown) on the same day. Otherwise either limit the steering riser to about +-1 m or accept 3 m-shift quality (FID roughly 70-95 in the driving benchmarks).
- Reconstruct the dense band about 30-50 m either side of the line, cover the far field with a low-LOD band out to the visible horizon, and draw the sky as an environment layer (cubemap, or Gaussians on a far sphere). Splats beyond about 100 m are seen from nearly one direction, so they cost a lot and add little.
- Overcast light makes appearance matching across blocks and passes much easier: sun position changes shadows, and ISP models (bilateral grid, PPISP) cannot fix shadows.

## 6 Multi-session

- Registration: COLMAP rigs model fixed sensor-from-rig poses, with "frames" of sensors exposed at the same time (VERIFIED [rig docs](https://colmap.github.io/rigs.html)). The 4.2.0 changelog (2026-08-31) fixes "pose-prior alignment and scale preservation for multi-camera rigs" and "pose-prior bundle adjustment changing rig poses configured as constant", and adds multi-component global mapping (VERIFIED [changelog](https://colmap.github.io/changelog.html)). With cm-level RTK priors on every frame, sessions share one ENU frame before matching, so joint registration is the same problem as single-session registration with spatial matching across passes (INFERRED). Use 4.2.1 (2026-09-29), not 4.1.1.
- Rig sync: rig frames assume simultaneous exposure. With 4 DSLRs, the GPS-time stamps give the per-camera offset. At 2 m/s, 10 ms of skew is 2 cm, so either trigger the cameras together or treat them as separate (non-rig) images with their own priors (INFERRED).
- Appearance: per-image embeddings and ISP models absorb exposure and white balance, not shadow position, wet versus dry road, or seasonal foliage (INFERRED; Block-NeRF names construction and seasonal vegetation as unhandled, VERIFIED [Block-NeRF](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.05263)).
- Practicality: merge lateral passes captured within about an hour of each other, so they share one appearance. Treat other sessions (another day or season) as separate builds of the route, or use them only for geometry and depth supervision. Merging different days into one splat set gives ghosted parked cars and flickering shadows (INFERRED).

## 7 Bandwidth

Assumptions (INFERRED unless marked):
- Bytes per splat: SOG is "~15-20x smaller than an equivalent PLY" (VERIFIED [SOG spec](https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/gaussian-splatting/formats/sog/)). A 3-band SH PLY is 62 float32 = 248 B per splat, so SOG is about 12-17 B. Use 15 B.
- LOD overhead: each metre of route is downloaded once per level it is shown at. With levels shrinking geometrically, total is about 2x the finest level, or 3x with Spark's 1.5 base.
- Density is unknown and must be measured on the 1 km MVP. Bracket it at 5k, 15k and 40k finest-level splats per metre of route (that is 0.5M, 1.5M and 4M per 100 m of a 60-100 m wide corridor).

| Finest density (splats/m) | Bytes per metre (15 B, 2x LOD) | Rate at 18 m/s | Rate at 8 m/s | 80 km route total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5,000 | 150 KB | 2.7 MB/s = 22 Mbit/s | 10 Mbit/s | 12 GB |
| 15,000 | 450 KB | 8.1 MB/s = 65 Mbit/s | 29 Mbit/s | 36 GB |
| 40,000 | 1.2 MB | 21.6 MB/s = 173 Mbit/s | 77 Mbit/s | 96 GB |

Look-ahead: the finest level must be resident from about 30 m ahead (its LOD distance) plus a buffer to absorb network stalls. A 10 s buffer at 18 m/s is 180 m, so about 210 m ahead at full detail, with coarser levels further out. Memory for the window is small: 210 m x 15k splats/m = 3.2M splats, about 47 MB compressed (INFERRED). Because the route is known and fixed, the robust design is to download (or progressively pre-cache) the whole ride's segments before and during the slow parts, and treat live streaming as a fallback. Browser storage quotas on Quest Browser are UNVERIFIED and need a test.

## 8 Implications for the pipeline (recommended design, INFERRED)

1. **Coordinates**: one route ENU frame from RTK for SfM; then per-segment local origins (about every 1 km) for export and rendering.
2. **SfM**: COLMAP 4.2.1 with a rig plus GNSS pose priors, sequential and spatial matching, and all lateral passes from the same session in one model.
3. **Training blocks**: cut along route arclength into blocks of about 100 m of route, each with 25-50 m overlap on both ends (Block-NeRF-style roughly 50% overlap is the safe end). Assign cameras that see the block, not only those inside it. Train each block on one GPU with gsplat MCMC (`cap_max` from the density budget), antialiased mode, PPISP plus EXIF exposure, per-image masks for dynamic objects and the rider, monocular depth or a road-plane prior, and a separate sky model.
4. **Seams**: after training, trim each block to its arclength ownership interval (an H3DGS-style nearest-chunk rule). Overlap during training is what makes the cut seamless, so no runtime blending is needed if appearance is shared. Fix the render exposure to one reference across the route.
5. **LOD and packaging**: concatenate trimmed blocks into roughly 1 km segments and run splat-transform (`--decimate-adaptive` or per-level inputs, `--lod-chunk-extent` about 16 m) to Streamed SOG, or Spark `build-lod` to `.RAD`. Keep the sky and far field as the `environment` layer. Publish a Quest tier (finest level capped at about 5k splats/m, SH degree reduced with `-H`) and a PCVR tier (full).
6. **Runtime**: Quest 3 global budget about 0.4M at 72 Hz with fixed foveation; PCVR 2-4M. Cut detail behind the rider.
7. **Steering riser**: bound lateral movement to the band that was actually captured (about 1 m beyond the outermost pass).
8. **Licensing**: no Inria, NVIDIA research or CC-NC code in the product path. Generative repair (Difix-class) is research-only today, so keep it out of production unless a commercial model is licensed.

Trade-offs: shorter blocks parallelise better but give more seams, and overlap costs repeated training (50% overlap is about 1.5-2x the GPU time). A lower Quest density cuts bandwidth and frame time but blurs the road texture near the wheel, which is what the rider looks at.
